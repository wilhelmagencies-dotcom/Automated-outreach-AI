## 2. Test window

  On day 7, judge which ads bring the right buyers. Leads in the 3 to 10 mil range come in
  slowly, and their quality only shows after the update calls, so 3 days is too short to
  judge quality.
- Carl decides.

## 4. Experimental ads

- Each one is unique. No copies of old winners.
- Each one is built on WHY past ads worked, not WHAT they did. Write down the reason for each
  winner and each loser, and build new ideas from that.
- Target buyer: knows what they want, decides fast, knows the market, and has the budget the
  client wants.
- The knowledge gets sharper with every result, per client.
- This applies to current clients, the new ads and all future ads.

## 5. New client: where the knowledge comes from (by budget range)

| Situation | Normal ads | Experimental ads |
|---|---|---|
| One existing client targets the same budget | Copy that client's normal ads that are working right now, tailored to the new client with small differences | Knowledge from that client |
| Several existing clients target the same budget | A mix of the normal ads working right now for those clients, tailored | Knowledge pooled from ALL of them, not just one |
| No client targets that budget | First tell Carl. Then use the normal ads working right now for the client(s) with the closest budget | Knowledge from the closest-budget client(s), plus new ideas that fit this budget. Keep what works, drop what doesn't |

After launch, every client builds its own knowledge of what works for them specifically.
